Prey proposals

What the shoggoths hunt: six designs, each a direct reference to a creature from public-domain science fiction. E, the zoogs, were adopted and are now the slimeverse's grazers.

The predator is a shoggoth now, so the prey should answer it: something the same imagination would put in the same landscape. Each is drawn in this world's vocabulary, a gel body with a 2px ink silhouette and per-beast variation from a hash, rather than in its original illustrator's.

On the sources: Wells's The First Men in the Moon (1901) and Burroughs's A Princess of Mars (1912) are unambiguously public domain in the US, so the mooncalf, the Selenite and the thoat need no further thought. Lovecraft's creatures are the complicated ones: At the Mountains of Madness (1936) and The Dream-Quest of Unknown Kadath (1943) fall in the window where US copyright had to be renewed, and the Mythos is treated as public domain in practice, but that rests on non-renewal rather than on age. The zoogs are the adopted design, so that check applies to them: they come from Dream-Quest, whose publication history is the one worth confirming. The other five stay here as the roads not taken.
