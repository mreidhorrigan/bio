Musebots

Musebots are software musicians: bots that listen to one another and play together. Here is what can be played, heard, and seen of them.

Play the instruments

Flutes, oboes, clarinets, saxophones, bassoons, brass, and bowed strings, each a physical model that runs in the browser. Set the breath, the lips, the bow, and the fingers by hand, or ask a musebot to play the instrument a piece.

Open the solo instrument tests

The page needs a recent desktop browser. Its terms and its engines' sources and licences are listed with it.

Compose a piece

Choose the players and the model of each instrument. The bots compose a piece for that band and play it on the physical models, in the browser. The page gives its score and parts, each part's MIDI 2.0, and its sound.

Open the band

The band is the last part of the solo instrument tests' page. An instrument set by hand there can join the band as it is set. Composing needs Chrome, Edge, or Safari.

Build a band

In the village on the home page, each signal tower houses one musebot. The towers on a page share one clock and one room, and their bots play together.

How it is built

  • Each bot is a program of its own. Bots share a clock and tell one another what they play and plan, on one machine, across a network, or in a browser.
  • The bots agree on a piece's meter, tempo, and harmony before its first sound. None of them needs a language model to perform.
  • Pitch is a floating-point number of semitones, so every simulated instrument is microtonal and can be retuned chord by chord.
  • The winds are played on OpenWInD's numerical scheme and the bowed strings on Artifastring, by Graham Percival. Each instrument's body is built from published measurements of real instruments where they exist.
  • The clarinet's body and its player's settings were found together, on the engine itself. Each of the 45 notes on its fingering chart sounds within a cent of its pitch at mezzo-forte.
  • The bots' planning core, the MIDI 2.0 codec, and the wind player are written in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly, so the same code runs on a desktop and in a browser.
